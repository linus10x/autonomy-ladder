Let your AI agents act, and still prove who was allowed to do what.

Why

Someone will ask what the agent was allowed to do.

If an agent can move money, deny a claim or place an order, someone will eventually ask what it was allowed to do, who granted that, and how you take it back. "The model decided" isn't an answer an examiner accepts.

We think regulated firms should be able to run autonomous agents without giving up accountability, the audit record, or a person's final say. That's the reason this work exists.

How

A0 to A4, earned one step at a time.

The ladder runs from A0, where the agent only reads and recommends, to A4, where it runs in production with a veto it can't switch off. Each step up has to be earned.

  • Authority is granted only against evidence, and it can be revoked. The revocation is recorded too.
  • A check that the agent can't argue with sits between the agent and anything that moves money or touches a customer.
  • Every decision is written to a hash-chained record, so tampering shows up.
  • The check that clears a write is independent of the agent, so it doesn't share the agent's failure modes.

The self-score reads your program by its weakest control, not its best one. Seven questions, about two minutes, and the result stays in your browser unless you choose to email yourself the brief.

What

A score, six libraries, and controls you can run.

Free self-score

Seven questions, a tier from A0 to A4. It stays in the browser unless you use the email form. No account and no card.

Six open libraries

One per regulated vertical, public, permissively licensed, and archived with a Zenodo DOI.

Runnable controls

A funds gate that denies a transfer unless the sanctions screen finished first, a tamper-evident decision ledger, and a forwardable audit packet you can wire into CI.

These are reference implementations you can read, run and adapt. They aren't a control running in any institution's production today, and they aren't legal advice.
